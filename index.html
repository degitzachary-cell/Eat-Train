Food database

—

The complete Australian Food Composition Database, Release 3 (FSANZ) is built in — every food, straight from the official nutrient profiles, per 100 g. The 213 foods FSANZ also publishes as liquids are held per 100 mL, which is the honest basis for a drink. Energy is the label figure, calculated with dietary fibre.

Supermarket products

AFCD covers generic foods, not the branded lines on a Coles shelf. For those, Open Food Facts is the open, barcode-level database — filter it by brand or country on their advanced search, download the result as CSV, and load it here. Tab-separated exports, sodium in grams and salt-only rows are all handled.

It is crowd-sourced, so coverage is patchy and the odd entry is wrong — imports stay under their own Packaged category so you always know which numbers came from FSANZ and which did not. For a single item, the panel on the box beats any database: use New food.

Product data from Open Food Facts, used under the Open Database License. Their API allows about ten searches a minute per person, so lookups are deliberately one tap at a time rather than as you type.

Off by default. Switched on, the search sheet offers a button to query Open Food Facts for whatever you typed — one request, on your tap, carrying the search text and nothing else. No account, no identifier, no history. Anything you pick is saved to this device so it works offline afterwards.
